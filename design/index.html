Grow Label — Visual Thesis & Design System
01 · Visual thesis

Grow Label — Website V2

The capacity ledger.

The observation

In a veterinary or dental group, lost revenue is not abstract. It is whitespace in a column — a gap in tomorrow’s day sheet — and a row never written into the ledger. The call that rang out at 4:52pm. The estimate accepted and never scheduled. The recall that lapsed eleven months ago.

So the site is built out of the two artefacts that operators already read every morning: the column and the ledger row. Nothing here is a dashboard illustration. It is the same object, at brand scale.

The two structural devices

The column

A slot unit of 46px generates the whole spatial rhythm. Filled slots are solid; open slots are hollow with a hairline. Vertical hairlines divide sections like a schedule grid.

The four-stage bar

Estimated → Booked → Attended → Collected. Four descending segments of one hue. The signature graphic is an act of financial honesty, not decoration.

Position

Grow Label shows the whole staircase, not the top step.

Why this wins the room

Every competitor quotes one revenue number. A buyer paying five figures a quarter has been burned by exactly that number. Leading with the four-stage decomposition converts the brief’s hardest constraint into the brand’s sharpest differentiator.

Deliberately not

No rounded feature cards. No purple gradient. No stock clinic photography. No glassmorphism. Radius is 0–2px throughout, because ledgers do not have rounded corners.

03 · Typography

Three faces, three jobs.

A high-contrast editorial serif carries the argument; a grotesque carries the detail; a monospace carries every number, label and section index. Neither Inter nor Poppins appears anywhere.

Display

Instrument Serif · 400 + Italic
Two static files. Set oversized at −0.03em with a 0.94 leading. The italic is a semantic device: it marks the recovered half of every sentence.

04 · Grid & components

A ruled page, not a card deck.

12 columns on a 46px slot unit. Hairline rules do all the dividing work; elevation is used almost nowhere. Corner radius is 0, except 2px on controls where a hit target needs softening.

05 · Motion language

Three verbs. Nothing else.

Every animation on the site is one of three moves, and each one restates the product argument. If a proposed effect is not one of these three, it does not ship.

Verb 01 — Detect

Opacity 0→1 with a 4px rise, staggered 40ms. Marks appear where they actually are: scattered, off-grid, low confidence. 340ms, ease-out.

Verb 02 — Consolidate

translate3d only, to a shared x-position on the column grid. 680ms, ease-settle, staggered from the outside in. This is the product: unstructured signal becomes a queue.

Verb 03 — Settle

Bars grow left-to-right in sequence while their figures count up. 1100ms total. The descending staircase is the honesty argument, animated.

Budget

No animation library. IntersectionObserver + CSS custom-property transitions, with one rAF-throttled scroll-progress hook for the pinned sequence. Compositor properties only: transform, opacity, clip-path.

Never

No smooth-scroll hijack. No compulsory intro. No WebGL. No autoplay video. Nothing animates above the fold before the hero text is painted.

Reduced motion

Every sequence has an authored final state, not a disabled one. Under prefers-reduced-motion the consolidated grid and the completed staircase render immediately; durations collapse to 1ms.
