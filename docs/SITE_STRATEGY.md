# Site strategy

## The commercial problem this site solves

Grow Label sells to organisations that already measure capacity and already
argue about how it gets filled. At several thousand pounds or dollars a month,
the purchase is not made on enthusiasm; it is made by someone who has to defend
the decision afterwards.

That buyer has almost certainly been shown a single recovered-revenue figure by
a previous supplier, and has almost certainly found it did not survive contact
with the finance function. **The website's central job is to be the one in this
category that does not do that.**

Everything else follows from that decision.

## Audience

| Who                    | What they arrive wanting                   | What convinces them                                             |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Owners and founders    | A number, quickly                          | The number, and an immediate account of what is behind it       |
| Operations directors   | The manual list work off their team        | A specific, bounded description of what gets automated          |
| Practice managers      | To not be handed another system to babysit | Named escalation triggers and explicit team-controlled limits   |
| Finance and commercial | Something that survives a board meeting    | Four separated stages, source-event traceability, stated limits |

The site is written for the finance reader and made legible to the others.
Where those pull apart, the finance reader wins — because that reader is the
one who kills the deal.

## The position

> Grow Label shows the whole staircase, not the top step.

Competitors quote one figure. Grow Label reports estimated, booked, attended and
collected separately and never adds them together. This is presented as the
product's central claim rather than a footnote, and it is the reason the
signature graphic on the homepage is a descending four-bar staircase.

The bet: financial honesty is a differentiator in a category whose buyers have
been burned by the opposite.

## Homepage narrative

Eight numbered beats, in a fixed order, each on an alternating dark or light
ground. The numbering is real: this is a sequence, and a reader who skips beat
four does not understand beat seven.

| Beat | Section                                            | The argument it makes                                                |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hero | Find the revenue your practice already earned      | Names the proposition, the sector and the action within five seconds |
| § 01 | A busy practice and a leaking one look identical   | Loss is invisible from the front desk, and here are its six forms    |
| § 02 | Detection is the entire job                        | What is read, what is not, and why an event needs a deadline         |
| § 03 | Four modules, four points                          | Answer, Respond, Retain, Reactivate, and where each one sits         |
| § 04 | One number is a claim. Four numbers are an account | The signature sequence. The differentiator, argued visually          |
| § 05 | The second return is hours back                    | Recovered time — the reason operations directors keep it             |
| § 06 | Every figure opens into the event                  | Traceability, attribution, confidence, duplicates, corrections       |
| § 07 | Veterinary and dental lose money differently       | Two sectors, two products, routed to their own pages                 |
| § 08 | Start with an assessment, not a contract           | The ask, with the obligation explicitly removed                      |

## Conversion model

One primary action across the whole site: **request a revenue-recovery
assessment**. It appears in the navigation, in the hero, at the end of every
route, and nowhere in a form that competes with itself.

The assessment is deliberately framed as a measurement exercise rather than a
trial:

- It reads a defined historical window of the client's own operational data.
- It returns a written view of where demand is being lost, at estimated value.
- Nothing is installed and nothing changes in the client's systems.
- The client keeps the analysis whether or not they proceed.

The `/contact` page states all four steps before showing a single field, and
states what the assessment does _not_ cover as prominently as what it does.
Removing the perceived commitment is the conversion lever; scarcity or urgency
would be the wrong instrument for this buyer.

## Secondary journeys

- **Sceptic** → `/methodology`, which includes a section named _What Grow Label
  cannot credibly claim_ and five questions to ask any provider in the category,
  explicitly including Grow Label.
- **Evaluator** → `/platform` for architecture and the data boundary, then the
  relevant `/modules/*` page for what a module does and where it stops.
- **Sector buyer** → `/industries/veterinary` or `/industries/dental`, which are
  written as two products rather than one page with the noun swapped.
- **Researcher** → `/insights`, three notes that argue the measurement position
  at length without asking for anything.

## What this site deliberately does not do

- No client names, logos, testimonials, case studies or partner marks. There are
  none to show, and inventing them is the fastest way to lose the finance reader.
- No benchmark, industry average or "practices typically recover" figure.
- No guarantee, expressed or implied.
- No gated content, no newsletter capture, no chat widget, no exit intent.
- No pricing page. At this deal size the number comes from the assessment.
- No cookie banner, because there are no cookies to consent to.

## How success is measured

The site cannot measure its own conversion — it has no analytics, by design at
this stage. The signal is the quality of what arrives through `/contact`: the
form asks for sector and site count precisely so an unqualified enquiry is
visible before anyone spends time on it.

If enquiries arrive that have clearly read `/methodology`, the strategy is
working.
